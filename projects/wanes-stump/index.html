Large Stump Excavation

Central Florida · Stump Removal · Featured composite above

Large stump excavation is the practical answer when stump grinding will not satisfy the site plan. Oversized root balls — the kind that need a chainsaw for scale reference — require digging, lifting, hauling, and backfill. This project documents that heavier workflow from before conditions through pulling the stump out of the hole, loading, haul-away, and verifying the weight of material removed.

Even after we cut over half of the stump off, it still weighed almost 5 tons by the time we hauled it off to the landfill. That scale is why excavation and dump capacity matter on jobs like this.

When size forces excavation

Diameter, species, and root flare change everything. A thirty-two-inch reference chainsaw in the frame is not a prop; it is scale. Large stumps can defeat consumer grinders and still leave massive subsurface wood. Excavation removes the obstruction so fencing, pads, or clean lawn become possible.

Composite walkthrough

Before: stump in place. Process: digging out the stump, the stump beside a person for scale, pulling it free, and the stump out of the hole. After: loading the stump and documenting the weight of what we hauled away. Numbers align with those captions in the gallery.

Backfill is part of “done”

Pulling a root ball without backfill plans leaves a dangerous void. Covering the hole and compacting in lifts (as conditions allow) returns the yard to usable grade. Owners comparing stump removal quotes should ask who brings fill and who takes the stump away — both cost real money.

Haul weight and disposal transparency

Documenting the weight of what was hauled away sounds obsessive until you have been surprised by dump fees. On this job the almost-5-ton haul after cutting the stump down is the clearest proof of how much mass grinding would have left in the ground. Transparency builds trust and educates prospects who only compared grinding price sheets online.
