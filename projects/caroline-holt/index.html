Storm Tree Cleanup

Central Florida · Storm Cleanup · Featured composite above

This project is a storm tree cleanup job — fallen and damaged trees processed after high winds, stumps excavated where needed, and the yard restored for normal use. The numbered before / process / after composite at the top is the executive summary. Before frames show the storm mess. The process column highlights cutting up the trees. After frames and the six-months-later check-in show the property once the debris was gone.

What storm cleanup should include

Storm response is more than dropping timber in a pile. A complete job usually needs controlled cutting, branch processing, haul-off, and a stump decision: grind, excavate, or leave. On this property, stumps were dug where the owner wanted usable ground rather than trip hazards and regrowth points.

Breaking Ground is equipment-forward. Chainsaws start the job; excavators finish the stubborn parts. That combination is why the gallery shows both cutting and digging — not only a hero shot of a tree on the ground.

Why this job lives under Storm Cleanup

These photos belong with hurricane and storm debris work because that is what they are. If you arrived from a general tree-removal browse, the same crew handles planned takedowns too — but this page documents post-storm clearing and recovery.

Six months later matters

Day-of chaos photos can hide whether haul-off and grade recovery were finished. Including a later look helps owners evaluate whether the contractor leaves a site that stays usable instead of a debris field that becomes the next project.

Central Florida access realities

Gates, soft sand, septic locations, irrigation, and neighbor fences change methods after a storm. Sometimes sectional dismantling is safer than a single directional fall. We plan that after looking at photos and, when needed, walking the site.
